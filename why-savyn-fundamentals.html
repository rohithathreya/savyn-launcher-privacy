Why Savyn: Fundamentals

A phone is the device that I carry everywhere, unlock, and use all the time. Whether that is to order food, talk to friends, catch up with what's happening in the world, or when I want to escape something uncomfortable. It is with me more than anything or anyone in the world. That's a fact.

As of today, my phone is a neutral, zero-memory device — it just facilitates any and all apps on it. It doesn't "remember" or "learn" who I am, what my life is aimed towards, what I am trying to do more of or less of. Unlock → pick an app → the app takes you on a ride.

With this one single device, I could be doing things that make my life definitively better in the long term — figuring out healthy recipes, working out and learning better techniques, watching lectures and content that help me grow in my domains, sleeping well. Or I could be making my long-term life objectively worse in the pursuit of quick dopamine hits — scrolling mindlessly, ordering junk food, escaping my real commitments.

We all know what we need to do — it ain't rocket science. I know I must eat healthy, work out, sleep well, and learn more and more about my domains, and life will be awesome. But it is far easier to say than do. Now the extremists might say: delete Instagram, delete YouTube, delete Swiggy. But it is the same Swiggy that I order healthy food on, the same YouTube I watch lectures on. And I am not so addicted to Instagram that I want to do away with it — it does help me stay connected with my friends and with emerging trends in society. What I want is to use all of these in a judicious way that I don't regret. This is also something we all know very well — that fun and work must be balanced. It is exactly why we can never stick to those screen-time apps or other cop-like apps.

Us knowing exactly how we want to live and interact with our phone is never enough. And that is because we have finite willpower. Every time we unlock our phones and actively choose not to tap that Instagram icon or that Swiggy icon, or make the decision to order healthy instead of McDonald's, we spend some of that limited willpower we get to have every day. Sadly, for most of us, that willpower — or energy, or whatever we want to call it — is never enough. We either go all in and gas out early in the day (and the rest of the day goes haywire), or we deploy it selectively.

This leakage of energy happens throughout the day, with each interaction on our phones as well as everything we do in real life. We know what we must do, but we have to fight the short-term gratification force in our minds. And eventually, it is always a losing battle — that is just biology.

Two kinds of knowledge

The key thing here is that we actually do know what we must do. But this knowledge sits entirely in our heads — Knowledge Type A.

We also know exactly what we end up doing instead. And this knowledge is very conveniently learnt and leveraged by all the apps on our phones — Knowledge Type B.

The phone claims neutral ground, in the sense that it neither learns nor adapts to Type A nor Type B. But the apps it facilitates all favor Type B. Which essentially means that, downstream, only Type B is reinforced. The type that is already strong inside your head is weaponized outside of it.

Cheap gates against B

Every external attempt at trying to give Type A space to grow requires you to translate it into apps you want versus apps you don't, time limits, hard locks. This deeply nuanced knowledge of what you must be doing to live your ideal life is forced into the language of "Instagram for 20 minutes," "lock my phone at 12 am," and other such cheap gates. This never augments or reinforces Type A. It only keeps Type B at the gate. And eventually it gets too frustrating to stick with.

That is because Type A was never deeply understood by any external system. It just got translated into cheap guardrails against Type B.

A device that understands your Type A

What if your phone — the device you own — developed and maintained a dynamic and deeply nuanced translation of Type A for you, used that to scour through the infinite action space that the app ecosystem enables, and then presented you with the right set of ideas, apps, links, and reminders at the right time?

The thing about Type A is that when you do act on it, it feels very good. But the starting is the bottleneck, as it competes with the highly lucrative Type B options. A device that understands your Type A and evolves with you — that knows where you are on your journey and where you want to get to — can significantly cut those Type B options from your interface. But not in a restrictive way. It has full knowledge of both Type A and Type B. So: the right thing, at the right time.

Point B toward A

And here is the part that lets this work without becoming yet another cop: Type B is not the enemy to be blocked. It is the most valuable signal on the phone.

The very patterns the apps learn in order to exploit you — the 11pm scroll, the stress-order, the avoidance reach — are the exact moments where Type A is about to lose. A device that understands those moments doesn't have to fight them. It can meet the real need underneath the reach — to wind down, to feel better, to take a break — with the version of it you won't regret. Same urge, better resolution, offered before the reach instead of a wall thrown up after it.

For the first time, the system that knows your weak moments most intimately would be working for you instead of against you. Not policing Type B — pointing it toward Type A.

Your phone already knows who you keep being. Savyn is the first layer that knows who you're trying to become — and quietly puts it in front of you at the moment it matters.
